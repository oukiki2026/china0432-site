## The essentials in 30 seconds

- **What it is:** The **Sacred Heart of Jesus Church** (吉林耶稣圣心堂), a complete **Gothic cathedral** built by French missionaries between **1917 and 1926**, on the north bank of the Songhua River near Jilin Bridge.
- **Why it matters:** For nearly 70 years it was the **cathedral of the entire Jilin Diocese**. Its 45-meter bell tower and cruciform stone plan make it the finest Western religious architecture in Northeast China.
- **Today:** A **national-level protected heritage site** (since 2013) and still an active church. Allow 1 hour, easy to combine with a riverfront walk.

## Ten years to build, in the middle of warlord China

In **1898**, two French priests of the Paris Foreign Missions Society, Fathers Lalouyer and Lemaire, arrived in Jilin from Changchun to preach. In **1902** they bought a plot by the river. Then they waited, because China in those years was in no condition for cathedral-building.

Construction finally began in **1917** and took a full **ten years**. Think about what those ten years contained: the fall of the Qing, warlord chaos, the Russian Revolution spilling across the border. Through all of it, French money and Chinese stonemasons raised a Gothic church on the Songhua. It was consecrated in **1926**.

The result is the real thing, not a pastiche. The plan is a **Latin cross**, 33 meters north to south and 27 meters across. The **bell tower rises 45 meters**. Everything is **brick and stone**: more than a dozen round stone columns over 20 meters tall inside, no wooden beams at all, stained-glass windows, five altars. The stone came from local quarries, cut and fitted by craftsmen who had never seen a Gothic vault before they built one.

If you know Beijing's **Xishiku Cathedral**, also French and also Gothic, you know the family resemblance. Jilin's is smaller and far less known, which is exactly why it rewards the detour.

## A cathedral that lost its title

From its consecration in 1926 until **1994**, this was the **cathedral church of the Jilin Diocese**, the seat of the bishop for all of Jilin Province. In 1994, after the death of Bishop Li, the diocesan administration moved to **Changchun**, and the cathedral title went with it to St. Teresa's Church there.

So the building kept everything except the name. It remains a parish church with regular services, and in **2013** it was listed as a **Major Historical and Cultural Site Protected at the National Level** (全国重点文物保护单位), which is China's highest heritage designation.

The twentieth century left marks. During the **Cultural Revolution** from 1966, the church was occupied and damaged. It was returned to the Catholic community and restored in the **1980s**, and religious life resumed. The stone survived; the continuity did too.

## Visiting today

The cathedral stands at **3 Songjiang Middle Road, Chuanying District** (船营区松江中路3号), by the **north end of Jilin Bridge**, an easy stop on any riverfront walk. See the [travel guide](/travel/jilin-city-travel-guide/) for the route.

- **What to see:** The west front with its twin-tower composition and central bell tower, the cruciform plan best appreciated from the side, the stone columns and stained glass inside. Trip.com reviewers rate it **4.8 out of 5**, unusually high for a single building.
- **It is an active church.** Dress respectfully, keep your voice down, and avoid visiting during Mass unless you intend to attend. Check mass times locally.
- **Photography:** Exterior photography is fine. Inside, follow posted rules and the lead of the congregation.
- **Combine it** with the riverfront and, for the full Qing-to-Republic arc, the [Jilin Arsenal](/heritage/jilin-arsenal/) across the water. One built guns for the dynasty, the other prayed through its fall.

Practical note: the building has undergone periods of renovation, and access policies can change. Confirm locally before making a special trip.

## FAQ

**Is it Catholic or Protestant?**
Catholic, Latin Rite, built by the French Paris Foreign Missions Society (巴黎外方传教会). The Sacred Heart devotion is a classic French Catholic dedication.

**Why did the cathedral move to Changchun?**
Administrative decision in 1994 after the bishop's death. Changchun is the provincial capital, and St. Teresa's Church there became the new cathedral. Jilin's church kept its heritage status and its congregation.

**How does it compare to other churches in China?**
It is one of the most architecturally complete Gothic churches in northern China. Southern China has more famous ones, but north of Beijing, Jilin's is in a class of its own for authenticity of style.

**Can non-Catholics visit?**
Yes, as respectful visitors outside service times. It is a heritage site as well as a church.
