## The essentials in 30 seconds

- **What happened:** On **March 8, 1976**, a fireball tore over Jilin and shattered 19 km up, raining **138 meteorites** across 500 square kilometers, the largest witnessed meteorite shower in recorded history.
- **The star:** **Jilin No. 1**, weighing **1,770 kg** — the largest single stony meteorite ever found on Earth. It's here, in this museum, and you can stand next to it.
- **The museum:** China's first purpose-built meteorite museum, on the south bank of the Songhua River. Half a day, easy to combine with a riverfront walk.

## The afternoon the sky fell

At about **3:02 PM on March 8, 1976**, villagers north of Jilin City heard what they first took for thunder, then looked up and saw daylight turn theatrical. A fireball "with an ear-splitting roar" crossed the sky, broke apart high in the atmosphere, and scattered stones across the frozen fields of Yongji County.

The numbers from that afternoon are still startling:

- **500 square kilometers** of strewn field: stones falling across an area the size of a mid-sized city.
- **138 intact specimens** recovered, plus over 3,000 fragments, totaling **more than 2,700 kg**.
- The largest crater was **2 meters across and 3 meters deep**, punching through **6.5 meters** of frozen soil. The impact registered like a **magnitude 1.7 earthquake**; witnesses reported a mushroom cloud and dirt thrown 150 meters.
- **Nobody was hurt.** No houses destroyed. For the largest rockfall in witnessed history, Jilin got off lightly.

If you remember the **Chelyabinsk meteor of 2013**, the Russian fireball caught on a thousand dashcams whose shockwave injured 1,500 people, Jilin is its stranger cousin. Chelyabinsk's rock exploded in the air and rained glass-shattering chaos on a city; Jilin's broke high and fell as recoverable stones onto empty farmland. Scientists dream of Chelyabinsk footage and Jilin specimens: one gave us the video, the other gave us the rocks.

## Why scientists still care

The Jilin meteorite is an **H5 chondrite**: a stony meteorite, the most common kind. This one is special for three reasons:

1. **It was witnessed.** Most meteorites are found years later, anonymous rocks in a desert. Jilin fell in front of thousands of people at a recorded second, 15:02:36, with its trajectory, breakup altitude, and strewn field all documented. That context makes every gram more valuable to science.
2. **It's ancient.** Analysis shows the parent asteroid was **4.6 billion years old**: older than Earth itself, a leftover building block from the solar system's formation that drifted in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter until its orbit decayed.
3. **It's the biggest of its kind.** Jilin No. 1, at 1,770 kg, is the **largest single stony meteorite known**, roughly the weight of a small car in one unbroken rock from space.

Small pieces of Jilin exist in museums worldwide: a couple hundred grams in London, a couple hundred in Washington. But the mountain stayed home.

## Inside the museum

The **Jilin Meteorite Museum** （吉林市陨石博物馆） sits on the south bank of the Songhua River, near Jilin Bridge, fittingly within sight of the river the city is named for.

- **Jilin No. 1**: the 1,770 kg main mass, displayed like the celebrity it is. Photos don't prepare you; it's the size of a large desk, pitted and blackened, and it fell out of the sky.
- **Jilin No. 2**: 126 kg, and **you're allowed to touch it**. Few museums on Earth let you lay hands on a witnessed fall.
- **Jilin No. 3** and 41 meteorite specimens from falls around the world, for comparison.
- Exhibits on how meteorites form, solar-system science, and the full documentary record of the 1976 shower — graphs, analysis, and the crater story.

Allow **2 hours**. The science exhibits are visual enough to enjoy with minimal Chinese, though an English-speaking guide (arranged via your hotel) deepens it considerably.

## Practical tips

- **Combine it** with a Songhua riverfront walk — the museum's riverside location makes this natural. See the [travel guide](/travel/jilin-city-travel-guide/) for the riverfront route.
- **Photography** is normally allowed; flash policies vary by hall — check signs.
- **Souvenirs:** the museum shop sells small certified meteorite fragments. They're genuine conversation pieces: a rock older than the planet, in your pocket. Prices vary; buy only from the official shop, as China's meteorite market is famously full of fakes.
- **With kids?** This is one of Jilin's best family stops: space rocks beat another temple for the under-12 crowd.

## FAQ

**Is it really the world's largest?**
The largest *single stony* meteorite, yes: 1,770 kg, and the largest *witnessed* stone fall by total recovered mass. (Iron meteorites can be bigger — Namibia's Hoba meteorite weighs about 60 tonnes — but that was a prehistoric fall; among stones that fell while people watched, Jilin stands alone.)

**Can I see where they fell?**
The strewn field is farmland in Yongji County, north of the city — there's no visitor center at the fall site itself. The museum is the experience.

**Is the museum English-friendly?**
Partially. Key labels have English, but the deeper science panels are Chinese-heavy. The rocks themselves need no translation.
