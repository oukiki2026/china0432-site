## The non-negotiables in 30 seconds

- **Temperatures:** December–February regularly sits at **−15 to −25°C**, colder with wind off the Songhua River.
- **The system:** three layers (thermal base + fleece/down mid + windproof down parka), and **no exposed skin** — face mask included.
- **Feet first:** insulated, waterproof boots with aggressive grip. Icy sidewalks injure more visitors than the cold does.
- **Buy locally:** heat packs, gloves and hats are cheap and excellent in Jilin — don't overpack what you can buy for less on arrival.

## Temperature reality

| Month | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| December | −18 to −8°C | Winter sets in; rime season begins |
| January | −25 to −15°C | Coldest month; best rime odds — colder than Moscow in January |
| February | −22 to −12°C | Still deep winter, slightly longer days |

These are air temperatures. The wind along the river can make it feel 5–10 degrees colder, and pre-dawn rime viewings (6–9 AM) are the coldest hours of the day. The good news: it's a **dry cold** — with the right layers, −20°C in Jilin feels more manageable than −5°C in damp Shanghai.

## The layering system

**Base layer (thermal underwear, top + bottom).** Merino wool or synthetic — not cotton, which holds sweat and freezes you. This single item makes the biggest difference.

**Mid layer.** Fleece jacket or a light down sweater. On milder days (−10°C) this plus the shell is enough for city walking.

**Outer layer.** A long, windproof, waterproof **down parka** that covers your thighs. Windproof matters more than the temperature rating — the river wind cuts through fashion coats instantly.

**Legs.** Thermal leggings under windproof trousers or jeans. One layer on legs is rarely enough below −20°C.

## Head to toe checklist

- [ ] **Hat** covering ears (or a balaclava for rime mornings)
- [ ] **Windproof face mask** — frostbite on exposed cheeks is a real risk, not a scare story
- [ ] **Gloves × 2:** thin touchscreen pair + thick insulated pair for evenings/dawn
- [ ] **Insulated waterproof boots** with deep tread — the #1 item; sneakers are a hospital visit waiting to happen
- [ ] **Wool socks** (2–3 pairs; feet sweat, then freeze)
- [ ] **Heat packs** (*nuan tie* 暖贴): disposable warmers for pockets, boots and gloves — sold everywhere in Jilin for a few yuan
- [ ] **Sunglasses:** snow and ice glare on sunny days is intense
- [ ] **Scarf:** seals the gap between hat and coat

## Electronics in extreme cold

- **Phone batteries** die fast below −15°C. Keep your phone in an **inside pocket**, against your body — not in a coat outer pocket or handbag.
- **Camera batteries:** same rule. Carry at least one spare, warm.
- **Power bank** kept warm works; a frozen one doesn't. Charge everything overnight.
- **Touchscreens** get sluggish in the cold — set up offline maps and translation packs *before* going out.

## Buy it in Jilin (cheap and good)

Don't waste luggage space on everything. Jilin's winter gear is excellent value:

- **Heat packs, hats, gloves, scarves** — street stalls and supermarkets, a few yuan each
- **Thermal underwear** — ubiquitous and cheap
- **Lip balm and heavy moisturizer** — the dry cold cracks skin fast; buy locally, apply constantly

What to bring from home: your **boots** (fit matters, don't gamble) and your **parka** if you own a good one — quality outerwear is worth packing.

## The indoor surprise

Northern China has **central heating** from roughly November to March, and it's fierce — hotel rooms, restaurants and malls sit at 22–25°C while it's −25°C outside. Dress in **layers you can shed**: you'll strip down indoors and bundle up at every doorway. A mid-layer with a full zip beats a pullover you have to wrestle off in a restaurant.

## FAQ

**Is −25°C as bad as it sounds?**
With proper layers, no — dry cold is manageable, and you'll be moving between heated indoors constantly. With jeans and sneakers, yes — genuinely dangerous.

**Can I rent winter clothing in Jilin?**
Ski resorts (like Beidahu) rent ski wear, but there's no city-wide clothing rental. Buy the cheap stuff locally instead.

**What about medications?**
Cold doesn't affect most medications, but inhalers and insulin are temperature-sensitive — keep them warm, inside your coat.

**Do I need special gear for rime photography at dawn?**
Beyond the clothing above: a tripod (you won't hold steady while shivering), spare batteries warm inside your coat, and a lens cloth — your breath will fog the lens between shots.
