## The essentials in 30 seconds

- **What:** **Beidahu Ski Resort （北大湖滑雪场）** — one of China's top ski mountains, in the foothills of the Changbai Mountains, about an hour from Jilin City.
- **The stats:** **870 m vertical drop**, 27 trails spanning 48 km, 7 high-speed cable cars, and a ~160-day snow season (per resort listings — conditions vary by year).
- **The snow:** famously **dry powder** — the mountain sits sheltered on three sides, so there's little wind and the snow stays soft.
- **The headline for Western skiers:** Beidahu is on the **Ikon Pass** — the same pass that opens Aspen, Zermatt, and Niseko now opens a mountain in Jilin.
- **Season:** typically late November to March/April, snow-dependent. **It's October now — this season's planning starts today.**

## Why ski here instead of somewhere famous

The Ikon Pass listing is the tell: the international ski industry has decided this mountain belongs in the same conversation as the big names. The case for Beidahu:

- **Powder without the wind.** Mountains on three sides mean calm, cold air and dry, soft snow — the resort's nickname translates roughly as "powder snow heaven."
- **Real vertical.** 870 m of drop is serious by Asian standards — comparable to many well-known Japanese resorts, with long fall-line runs.
- **Olympic pedigree.** The resort hosted the **6th Asian Winter Games (2007)**, multiple National Winter Games, and FIS World Cup events. The terrain was good enough for that; it's good enough for you.
- **Night skiing.** Beidahu is billed as China's largest night-skiing destination — quiet slopes under the lights, a completely different mountain after dark.
- **The difference:** you won't find this combination anywhere else — dry Manchurian powder, rime-ice forests on the ridgelines, and then *iron pot stew* （铁锅炖） slow-cooked over a wood fire for dinner. Aspen can't do that.

## Getting there

- **From downtown Jilin:** ~53 km, roughly an hour by car. Hotel-arranged transfers, Didi, or the resort's shuttle buses in season.
- **From Changchun Longjia Airport (CGQ):** ~126 km — arrange a transfer in advance rather than improvising at arrivals.
- **Day trip or overnight?** Both work. Day-tripping from Jilin City is easy; staying at the resort (see below) buys you first lifts and night skiing.

## Terrain: who it's for

- **Beginners:** wide, gentle learning zones with magic carpets — the resort is known as one of Asia's more approachable mountains for first-timers. Lessons are available; book ahead on winter weekends.
- **Intermediates:** the sweet spot — long, groomed cruisers with real vertical.
- **Advanced:** steep pitches up to 30°+ on the upper mountain, plus tree-lined powder stashes after fresh snow (locals point to the Gangliang and Qingyun areas).

Trail counts and grooming change season to season — treat any specific trail map as a starting sketch, not a contract.

## Tickets, rental & lessons

- **Lift tickets:** priced per resort listings and seasonal — **check current prices before you go**; Chinese New Year and winter weekends cost more and sell out.
- **Rental:** full ski and snowboard rental on-site, including the large ski service center.
- **Lessons:** available in Mandarin; English-speaking instructors are not guaranteed — a translation app and a sense of humor help. If you're a complete beginner, consider one lesson before free-skiing.
- **What to wear:** see our [winter packing list](/travel/winter-packing/) — resort cold is real cold.

## Where to stay

- **At the resort:** Club Med Beidahu, Holiday Inn Resort Beidahu, and several other hotels — ski-in convenience, hot springs nearby, and night skiing on your doorstep. Book early for holidays.
- **In Jilin City:** cheaper, more dining options, and you can pair skiing with the city's rime ice and food scene. See [where to stay in Jilin](/travel/where-to-stay-in-jilin/).

## Beyond the slopes

- **Hot springs** at the resort — the correct end to a ski day.
- **Iron pot stew （铁锅炖）** — Northeastern comfort food slow-cooked over fire; the definitive Beidahu dinner.
- **Rime from the gondola** — ride up on a clear morning and the forests below wear the same frost Jilin is famous for.
- **Night skiing** — if your legs survive the day.

## The killer combo: ski + rime in one trip

This is the winter itinerary nobody else is selling you: **ski Beidahu by day, chase rime ice at dawn.** The resort is an hour from Jilin City; [Rime Island](/travel/rime-ice-guide/) is an hour north. Base yourself in the city for 3–4 days in December–February: dawn at the island, days on the mountain, nights eating your way through the [food guide](/taste/what-to-eat-in-jilin/). That's a winter trip with no equivalent anywhere else in China.

## Homegrown heroes: Jilin City's Olympic champions

Jilin doesn't just host winter sports — it produces winter Olympians. Two of China's most famous were born right here in Jilin City:

- **苏翊鸣 (Su Yiming)** — born in Jilin City in 2004, on skis (well, a snowboard) from age 4. Beijing 2022: **gold in snowboard Big Air** — China's first Olympic snowboarding champion — plus slopestyle silver. Milan-Cortina 2026: **slopestyle gold** (on his 22nd birthday) and Big Air bronze. Before any of that, he was a child actor, playing young Jiang Shuanzi in Tsui Hark's *The Taking of Tiger Mountain* — a film set, fittingly, in snowy Manchurian mountains.
- **李坚柔 (Li Jianrou)** — born in Jilin City in 1986. Sochi 2014: one of the most dramatic golds in short-track history. In the women's 500m final, the three skaters ahead of her all crashed out; she skated through the chaos for **China's first gold of those Games**.

This is the winter-sports culture you're visiting: the same mountains, the same −25°C mornings that made them. When you ride Beidahu's gondola up through the rime forest, you're on hometown snow.

## Practical tips

- **Book holidays early.** Chinese New Year week sells out resort hotels — weeks ahead, not days.
- **Weekdays > weekends** for empty slopes, everywhere in the world, Jilin included.
- **Altitude:** the top sits around 1,357 m — noticeable if you live at sea level, but not a medical event. Hydrate.
- **Payment:** Alipay/WeChat Pay country-wide, including the resort. Set them up before arrival.
- **Language:** minimal English outside Club Med and the Holiday Inn. Have your hotel's Chinese address screenshotted and a translation app ready.

## FAQ

**I've never skied — is Beidahu okay for beginners?**
Yes — wide beginner terrain, magic carpets, and lessons. It's genuinely one of the friendlier big mountains in Asia to learn on.

**Can I visit on a day trip from Jilin City?**
Easily — about an hour each way. But an overnight unlocks night skiing and first lifts.

**When does the season start?**
Typically late November, snow-dependent, running to March/April. Early season means thinner coverage; January–February is peak.

**Is it expensive compared to Japan or Europe?**
Generally much cheaper — lift tickets, rental, lessons, and lodging all run well below Niseko or Alpine prices. Confirm current rates before budgeting.

**Can I combine skiing with Harbin's Ice Festival?**
Ambitious but doable in a long trip — they're different provinces with a high-speed rail link via Changchun. That's a two-center winter expedition, not a weekend.
